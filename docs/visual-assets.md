# Visual assets

The immersive player renders transparent ambient overlays and transparent song motifs as separate layers above the animated collection color. Every collection also owns a distinct transparent fallback motif that is used only when a track-specific motif is absent or unavailable. Ambient overlays may contain only sparse abstract particles such as star points, rain dashes, bubbles, fireflies, or embers; they must not contain a scene, landscape, room, animal, instrument, or other principal motif. Background overlays, fallback motifs, and track motifs must be different files; neither collection-level image may be assigned as a track motif. `PlaybackVisualCatalog` owns the animated collection colors and packaged emergency fallback. The catalog and remotely updateable artwork manifest provide independently validated PNG references for all three remote layers.

`sleepy_bear_moon.png` and `sleepy_lamb_star.png` were generated for this project with OpenAI image generation on 2026-09-09. The collection-specific assets and the later lullaby motifs were generated for this project with OpenAI image generation in September 2026. They contain no third-party source artwork or embedded text and are distributed under the repository's MIT license.

The first versioned PNG set is published in the [artwork-v1 GitHub release](https://github.com/jochenwezel/open-sleep-music/releases/tag/artwork-v1). Collection fallbacks and the title-specific forest series are published in `artwork-v2`; the transparent ambient overlays are published in `artwork-v3`. Every catalog track carries HTTPS download URLs, portable cache filenames, and SHA-256 checksums. The app downloads and validates artwork independently from audio and falls back silently from a missing title motif to its collection fallback. Only `sleepy_lamb_star.png` remains packaged as the final offline emergency image; concrete collection and song artwork is not bundled into the APK.

The mutable [artwork-catalog release](https://github.com/jochenwezel/open-sleep-music/releases/tag/artwork-catalog) is the update channel used by installed apps. Its `artwork-catalog.json` can point existing track IDs to newer versioned background and optional song-motif releases and checksums without requiring an application update. The app caches the last valid manifest and falls back to the metadata embedded in its own media catalog when the channel is unavailable or invalid. A manifest entry must supply all three song-motif fields or none of them. Regenerate the manifest with `tools/Export-ArtworkManifest.ps1` after changing artwork assignments.

All five toddler lullabies and all eight forest tracks have separately published song motifs. Forest motifs follow the recording context: tropical-rainforest titles use tropical vegetation and animals, never the temperate fox, conifers, or fly agarics from the earlier concept. Collections without a reviewed title motif display their own collection fallback instead. Only `sleepy_lamb_star.png` is packaged; it remains the guaranteed emergency fallback when no downloaded artwork layer is available.

The animated collection color transitions over 15 seconds per phase and remains visible through the transparent ambient overlay. The fireplace overlay keeps its subtle three-second opacity crossfade. A visible song motif is centered in both orientations and drifts vertically by only six device-independent pixels over 12 seconds. It may use up to 84% of the portrait width or 55% of the landscape width while remaining clear of the controls. Animation callbacks run at 20 frames per second to limit battery and rendering load, and all ambient animation is stopped with the page and disabled when **Reduce motion** is enabled. Motifs use a strong silhouette, no flashes, and no rapid movement. New artwork should remain readable at phone size in portrait and landscape, use transparent backgrounds, avoid text, and document its source and license here.
